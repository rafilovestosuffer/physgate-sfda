## Details

**On the CWRU BSF convention.** The physical ball-spin frequency (rotation of a ball about its own axis) is (D/2d)·f_r·(1−(d/D·cosφ)²). CWRU's tabulated value (4.7135×f_r for the 6205) equals **2×** that quantity, because a defect on a ball contacts both raceways once per ball revolution, producing two impacts per revolution. Diagnosticians therefore search the envelope spectrum at 2×BSF (ball-defect/ball-pass frequency), where even harmonics dominate — exactly as Smith & Randall report. Your protocol should state explicitly which convention you adopt (true ball spin vs. 2× ball spin) to avoid a factor-of-two mismatch against CWRU's table.

**On the JNU contradiction.** The direct literature contradiction is real and consequential. The authoritative resolution is the original Jiangnan University source (Li et al., Sensors 2013), whose authors built the rig. Their explicit statement — N205 (separable outer ring) for normal/outer/roller; NU205 (separable inner ring) for inner — should be treated as ground truth over any secondary reinterpretation, and the mechanical logic (you must separate the ring to expose a raceway for wire-cutting) confirms it.

**On Bio-SFDA's evidentiary status.** Only the abstract was retrievable from primary-adjacent SKKU/SciProfiles sources. All fine-grained specifics — EAGLE's formula/threshold/FAR control, its input representation, dataset/task/window details, per-task numbers beyond CWRU→PU, split protocol/leakage, named baselines, and fault-frequency handling — require the paywalled ScienceDirect full text and are currently unverified.
