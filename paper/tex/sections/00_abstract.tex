Conventional source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) benchmarks for rolling-bearing diagnosis can place the same physical bearings on both sides of a transfer. We re-evaluate source-free domain adaptation based on label reliability (SDALR) and source hypothesis transfer (SHOT) on the Paderborn bearing dataset in a paired design: one source model is adapted to the same bearings and to unseen bearings at the same target condition. Over ten pre-specified bearing splits, unseen bearings cost SDALR a median 36.2 percentage points (conditional 95\,\% interval 21.6--59.4; 10 of 10 splits) and SHOT 36.6 points. In post-hoc analyses, a given bearing loses a median 38.7 points when unseen rather than seen, and the gap is already present before adaptation (35.1 points). Non-adaptive forests and a fixed-condition control also lose accuracy; a second rig shows a similar loss, although specimen and bearing size change together. A forest's unseen-bearing accuracy rises from \SI{46}{\percent} to \SI{75}{\percent} as its source grows from one to four bearings per class, but training volume grows with it, so the cause is not isolated. An oracle pseudo-label filter recovers a median \SI{82}{\percent} of the loss. Envelope-based gates falsely flag 0 to 9 of 480 recordings from six healthy bearings, against 214 for a simplified raw-spectrum rule, but cover mainly larger-damage specimens. Inside SDALR the envelope threshold gate adds a median 4.5 points; in a post-hoc comparison, its verdicts used directly on the recordings it certifies add 8.7. Unseen-bearing claims therefore need bearing-disjoint targets, a source-only comparison and gate coverage reported by specimen.
