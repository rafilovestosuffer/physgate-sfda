Conventional source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) benchmarks for rolling-bearing diagnosis can place the same physical bearings on both sides of a transfer. We re-evaluate source-free domain adaptation based on label reliability (SDALR) on Paderborn in a paired design: one source model is adapted to the same bearings and to unseen bearings at the same target condition. Over ten pre-specified bearing splits, unseen bearings cost a median 36.2 percentage points (conditional 95\,\% bootstrap interval 25.0--56.3; 10 of 10 splits), and a given bearing loses a median 38.7 points when unseen rather than seen. The gap is already present before adaptation (35.1 points) and remains substantial afterwards. Non-adaptive forests and a fixed-condition control also lose accuracy; a second rig shows a loss of similar direction and size, although specimen and bearing size change together. A forest's unseen-bearing accuracy rises from \SI{46}{\percent} to \SI{75}{\percent} as its source grows from one to four bearings per class, with training volume increasing simultaneously. This is consistent with inadequate source coverage but does not isolate its cause. An oracle pseudo-label filter recovers a median \SI{82}{\percent} of the loss. Envelope-based gates falsely flag 0 to 9 of 480 recordings from six healthy bearings, against 214 for a raw-spectrum rule, while coverage is concentrated on larger-damage specimens. Inside adaptation the envelope threshold gate adds a median 4.5 points, as a transductive benefit concentrated on certified recordings. We identify the evidence needed to interpret such results.
