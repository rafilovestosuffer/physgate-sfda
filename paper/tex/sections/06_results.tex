\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
All confidence intervals (CIs) in this section are the order-statistic intervals of Section~\ref{sec:protocol}: for split-level medians they cover the median over the eligible source sets of the fixed Paderborn pool, and they are not uncertainty bounds for new bearings. Differences in the tables are computed from unrounded values.

\subsection{Reproduction}
\label{sec:res-repro}

Before changing anything we reproduced the published method from its released code on its own tasks. These tasks differ
from the rest of the paper in label space: the published Paderborn task has eight classes, each one physical bearing (K001,
KA04, KA15, KA22, KA30, KI14, KI17, KI21), so every target specimen is also a source specimen. On JNU the mean accuracy over
the six condition pairs is \SI{98.03}{\percent} against \SI{98.50}{\percent} reported; on Paderborn it is
\SI{96.20}{\percent} against \SI{96.78}{\percent}. The two hardest tasks bracket the published values over four seeds
(A3$\to$A2: $93.9\pm4.7$ against 97.84 reported; A1$\to$A2: $90.3\pm4.9$ against 87.03), and the seed standard deviation on
those tasks, about 5 points, exceeds many differences reported between competing methods in this literature. We treat the
method as reproduced and use the released hyperparameters unchanged. Everything after this subsection uses the
three-class label space with several bearings per class; the leaky baselines in the rest of this section are measured in that space, not
against the published number.

\label{sec:res-collapse}

Across the ten pre-specified random splits (Table~\ref{tab:collapse}, Fig.~\ref{fig:collapse}) SDALR loses a median of 36.2
points on bearing-disjoint targets (conditional 95\,\% CI 21.6--59.4, range 8.2--61.6), positive in every split. No single bearing carries it: in a post-hoc check with the windows of any one bearing dropped from every target, the median stays between 29.3 and 43.5 points, positive in at least 9 of 10 splits (Supplementary~S6). Which bearings happen to be in the source moves clean accuracy between \SI{36.4}{} and
\SI{74.0}{\percent}, illustrating the sensitivity of a performance estimate to the selected bearing set. On
the two mechanical folds (fold job, mean of the two directions) the leaky arm reaches \SI{94.1}{\percent} and the clean arm
\SI{56.8}{\percent}, a loss of 30.0
points with fold~A as source and 44.6 with fold~B; with fold~B as source all six leaky tasks exceed \SI{99.7}{\percent} while
all six clean tasks fall between \SI{55.27}{} and \SI{55.33}{\percent}. The released checkpoint policy, which consults target
labels, gives \SI{94.7}{\percent} against \SI{57.8}{\percent} on the folds: target-label selection is worth 0.6 points on leaky
and 1.0 points on clean targets on average (per task 0.0--4.1), so it does not account for the gap.

\subsubsection{The gap is there before adaptation}

A post-hoc analysis reads the batch-0 accuracy recorded in every adaptation
log, which is the source model's accuracy before any update. On the ten splits the source model alone already loses a median 35.1 points on unseen bearings
(conditional CI 21.2--55.0, 10 of 10; Table~\ref{tab:collapse}, column ``Source-only $\Delta$''). SDALR's adaptation then changes the gap
by a median $+4.7$ points (positive in 8 of 10): it raises leaky accuracy by a median 2.0 points and
lowers clean accuracy in 7 of 10 splits (median $-2.3$). On the folds adaptation adds 3.4 and 2.2 points on clean targets,
so the folds alone would suggest that adaptation helps. The gap therefore precedes adaptation and remains substantial afterwards. The 4.7-point median of paired gap changes is not the difference between the separate 36.2- and 35.1-point medians.

\subsubsection{The same bearing, seen and unseen}

Leaky and clean targets hold different bearings, so $\Delta_{\mathrm{leak}}$ also
contains the difference in difficulty between two bearing sets. Across the ten splits every bearing is in the source in some
splits and in the clean target in others, so its accuracy when seen can be compared post hoc with its accuracy when unseen
(Table~\ref{tab:bearings}, Fig.~\ref{fig:bearings}a). The within-bearing difference has a median of 38.7 points over the 17
bearings (descriptive order-statistic range 9.5--62.5): positive for 14 and tied for 3 (the healthy K003, K004 and K006, recognised perfectly either way); none is
negative. By class the median is 2.9 points for healthy specimens, 62.5 for outer-race and 49.0 for inner-race ones. Two
outer-race bearings, KA16 and KA30, are never correctly classified by SDALR when unseen. The seen and unseen scores come from
different adaptation runs and bearings share splits, so this contrast is descriptive; it holds the specimen fixed, which
the leaky--clean contrast cannot.

\subsection{Bearing-disjoint losses in non-adaptive controls and a second rig}
\label{sec:res-second}

\input{tables/t4_methods}

Table~\ref{tab:methods} places SDALR beside SHOT and non-adaptive controls under the same bearing splits.

\subsubsection{SHOT}

Run from the same source checkpoints, SHOT loses a median 36.6 points on bearing-disjoint targets (conditional CI 19.7--62.0; positive in 10 of 10 splits), meeting the pre-specified rule (median at least 10 points, sign-flip $p<0.05$). Its source-only gap is the 35.1 points of the shared source model; adaptation raises the gap by a median 3.9 points and lowers clean accuracy by a median 2.8 points (leaky accuracy changes by a median 2.2). On bearing-disjoint targets SHOT is lower than SDALR by a median 1.4 points (4 of 10 splits higher). SHOT is SDALR's own base rather than an independent family, so the two do not establish method generality.

\subsubsection{Non-adaptive forests}

Random forests \cite{pedregosa2011sklearn} trained on the same source windows with no adaptation at all lose a median 22.7
points on ten time-domain statistics (conditional CI 15.0--35.9) and 32.1 points on the combined feature set (conditional CI 24.1--43.9), each in 10
of 10 splits. On bearing-disjoint targets the time-statistics forest is more accurate than
adapted SDALR by a median 8.9 points (conditional CI $-0.2$ to 16.7, 8 of 10 splits).

\subsubsection{A second rig}

The result replicates on a second rig. On HUST bearing (Fig.~\ref{fig:collapse}b; per-split values and source bearing types in Supplementary~S6) mean leaky accuracy
is \SI{98.7}{\percent} and mean clean accuracy \SI{59.6}{\percent}, a median split-level loss of 33.5 points (range 25.8--61.1), positive in all six splits. Each HUST bearing type is a different size, so the clean target changes geometry as well as specimen. This supports the same direction and a similar observed magnitude within a second fixed pool; it does not isolate a specimen effect. The pre-specified replication-rule assessment is given in Supplementary~S6.

\subsubsection{Without any condition shift}

Every task considered so far crosses operating conditions. In a pre-specified control the time-
statistics forest is trained, at each condition separately, on half of the source bearings' recordings and tested at the
\emph{same} condition on the held-out recordings of the same bearings and on recordings of the complementary bearings;
recordings never cross the split, which is the separation used by recording-level benchmarks \cite{knap2026leakagesafe} and
the repetition-wise split of Vieira et al.\ \cite{vieira2026}. Balanced accuracy falls by a median of 29.7 points (conditional CI
16.5--42.0, 10 of 10), from a median \SI{94}{\percent} on held-out recordings of seen bearings to
\SI{65}{\percent} on unseen ones. A large bearing-disjoint loss can therefore occur without changing operating condition; this control and the cross-condition results use different accuracy summaries and are not an equivalence comparison. A post-hoc breakdown by class
locates the loss: healthy recall barely moves (median 99.9 to \SI{98.2}{\percent}); inner-race recall, where the principal
damage of every bearing is fatigue pitting (KI04 and KI14 also carry outer-ring indentations, Section~\ref{sec:data}
\cite{lessmeier2016}), falls by a median 28.5 points; outer-race recall, which mixes pitting with plastic deformation from
indentations, falls by 66 points, and on unseen indentation bearings (KA15, KA30) it is a median \SI{23}{\percent} against
\SI{64}{\percent} on pitting ones (KA04, KA16, KA22; medians over the eight and ten target sets that contain such bearings,
folds included). With two indentation and three pitting specimens, this contrast separates specimens as much as
mechanisms. This control uses a forest; SDALR was not run at a fixed condition.

\subsection{Specimen variation and source coverage}
\label{sec:res-mechanism}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig3_bearings.pdf}
\caption{Accuracy of each bearing when seen and unseen, and the source-diversity curve. (a) Each row compares, post hoc, SDALR's window accuracy on one Paderborn bearing over the ten splits when the bearing was in
the source and when it was unseen, with the four bearings the envelope gate can certify in amber. (b) In the pre-specified
source-diversity curve, a random forest on ten time statistics is trained at one condition on one to four bearings per
class and scored by balanced accuracy on held-out recordings of its training bearings and on one unseen bearing per class.
Points are medians and bands interquartile ranges over 30 test triples; the dotted line is the three-class chance level.}
\label{fig:bearings}
\end{figure}

Specimen-associated features and inadequate coverage of damage variation can both contribute to poor transfer, and these explanations are not mutually exclusive. Probes and a source-expansion curve describe the available information and the sensitivity to the training set.

\subsubsection{Specimens are identifiable, but reliance on identity is not established}

A logistic-regression probe fitted within each
class on record-disjoint halves identifies the physical bearing in 507 of 508 held-out recordings from the frozen SDALR source
features and in 508 of 508 from ten elementary time statistics (pooled within-class chance \SI{35}{\percent}); the 508 recordings are the held-out half
of the 1020 source recordings of the six source models. The identification survives removing the signal mean, the statistic
most tied to sensor offset (508 of 508); the standard deviation alone identifies 477. A simple within-session drift control is much less discriminative: the
same probe asked to separate the first ten from the last ten recordings of one bearing succeeds on 285 of 508 (chance 254).
But healthy specimens are identified as perfectly as faulty ones (180 of 180) and still transfer (Table~\ref{tab:bearings}).
Identity is available in these signals; whether a classifier relies on it is a different question.

\subsubsection{More source specimens transfer better}

In a pre-specified curve the forest was trained on one to four source
bearings per class and tested on a fixed unseen bearing per class, for 30 test triples at each of the three conditions
(Fig.~\ref{fig:bearings}b). Balanced accuracy on the unseen bearings rises from a median \SI{45.9}{\percent} with one
specimen per class to 57.9, 66.9 and \SI{74.6}{\percent} with two, three and four, while accuracy on held-out recordings of
the training bearings stays at 95--100\,\%. The paired gain from one to four specimens has a median of 15.4 points over the
triples (25 of 30 positive). The Monte Carlo calculation is in Supplementary~S6.

The intervention increases both source diversity and training volume: median training-window counts are 1043, 2129, 3210 and 4265 as the number of bearings per class rises from one to four. Source subsets at successive sizes were drawn separately rather than nested. The curve is consistent with inadequate source coverage, as in the supervised observations of Vieira et al.\ \cite{vieira2026}, but does not isolate diversity from sample count or rule out reliance on specimen-associated features. It is a forest result, not a source-diversity intervention on SDALR. The 28.8-point difference between endpoint medians and the 15.4-point median paired gain are different summaries of the same outcomes.

\subsubsection{Labelling whole specimens is not a sign of failure}

In a post-hoc purity analysis, an adapted model that misclassifies an unseen bearing usually
does so for all its windows: with fold~B as source every clean bearing receives a single label on at least
\SI{99.9}{\percent} of its windows over the six tasks (in the gating job's per-window predictions), and in four of the tasks
the resulting \SI{55.33}{\percent} is exact arithmetic (three healthy and two fault bearings right,
$(2\,000 + 660 + 660)/6\,000$).
But block labelling is equally common when the bearing was seen: per task, \SI{90}{\percent} of seen and \SI{89}{\percent} of
unseen bearing--task pairs receive one label for at least \SI{99}{\percent} of their windows, and the fold~A direction is not
pure (K004 0.67, KI17 0.56). A specimen's windows are similar to each other, so a classifier decides the specimen as a whole;
the failure is in which label it gives, not in how it gives it. In a post-hoc count, that label is almost never `healthy': unseen damaged
bearings are labelled healthy on at most \SI{5.8}{\percent} of their windows, and the error is a confusion between the two
fault families, which takes a median \SI{69}{\percent} of an unseen damaged bearing's windows (Supplementary~S6).

\subsubsection{The kinematic control is sensitive to class weighting}

The 2048-sample windows poorly resolve the harmonic structure used by the gates (Section~\ref{sec:windows}). A pre-specified forest instead uses 13 full-recording kinematic features: floor-normalised envelope maxima at the specimen's first five outer- and inner-race harmonics and shaft orders 1--3. Its ordinary-accuracy gap has median 8.4 points (conditional CI 2.4--30.8; 9 of 10 splits), with median leaky and clean accuracies of \SI{65.8}{} and \SI{56.1}{\percent}. This meets the pre-specified rule, a median gap below 10 points in record-level ordinary accuracy, but is not evidence of equivalence or reliable transfer.

Unlike the class-balanced window comparisons, this record-level clean target contains three healthy, three inner-race and two outer-race bearings. Its balanced accuracy gives a median gap of 10.4 points (conditional CI 5.6--26.7; 10 of 10 positive), median clean balanced accuracy \SI{54.4}{\percent}, and mean \SI{52.3}{\percent} (Table~\ref{tab:methods}; Supplementary~S6). Under balanced accuracy the same threshold is not met, so the conclusion depends on class weighting. The representation has a smaller observed gap than the time-statistics forest, but the wide interval, low accuracy and simultaneous changes in duration and aggregation prevent a claim of robust transfer or an isolated representation effect. Concurrent source-dependent adaptation work also finds representation important on held-out Paderborn bearings \cite{nagaswetha2026}.

\subsection{How much an oracle pseudo-label filter recovers}
\label{sec:res-decomp}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig4_decomposition.pdf}
\caption{Oracle decomposition of the unseen-bearing loss. Each bar divides the collapse $\Delta_{\mathrm{leak}}$ of one random split by Decomposition~\eqref{eq:decomp}
into the part the oracle pseudo-label filter recovers and the part it does not, with the recovered share above the bar.}
\label{fig:decomp}
\end{figure}

On the ten random splits, where all four arms ran in one job, the oracle filter recovers a median \SI{82}{\percent} of the
collapse (conditional CI 60--98, interquartile range 70--\SI{90}{\percent}, Fig.~\ref{fig:decomp}). The share varies: at
\SI{48}{\percent} on S03 half the collapse is not recovered, while on S04, the mildest collapse (8.2 points), the oracle
arm exceeds the leaky arm, a share of \SI{222}{\percent}. The fold-level decomposition is not reported, because the gating job
that produced the oracle arm has no leaky arm (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}).

The oracle withholds every incorrect pseudo-label and keeps every correct one; it is the keep-or-withhold counterpart of the
perfect-pseudo-label level of Schlachter et al.\ \cite{schlachter2025}. It is a reference for filters acting on this
method's pseudo-label step (the family to which all four gates belong), not a proven bound, and it says nothing about
methods that relabel, reweight continuously or change the representation. The part it does not recover mixes what the
source model carries with the difference in difficulty between the two bearing sets, as S04 shows. With that reading,
correct pseudo-labels alone would close most of the gap on this rig; the question for physics gating is how many correct labels it can retain without retaining incorrect ones.

\subsubsection{Healthy false acceptance}

Gate behaviour is characterised on all 2319 single-fault and healthy Paderborn recordings: 29 physical
bearings (6 healthy, 11 real-damage, 12 artificial-damage) at four operating conditions, 80 recordings per bearing less one distributed file that cannot be read (N15\_M01\_F10\_KA08\_2). At the operating points fixed
before evaluation (Table~\ref{tab:gates}) the envelope threshold rule accepts a fault on 1 of 480 healthy recordings from six bearings (80 recordings each), the alias-guarded comb on none, the surrogate-null comb on 9, and the raw-spectrum band rule on 214. Their fault verdicts on faulty recordings name the wrong family 8 times in 374 for the envelope rule, 3 in 315 for the alias-guarded comb, 21 in 330 for the surrogate-null comb and 147 in 576 for the raw-spectrum rule. Per-bearing counts are shown alongside these totals; assumption-dependent intervals are in Supplementary~S6. Sweeping each gate's parameter
(Fig.~\ref{fig:roc}a) shows the envelope rule on or above the other measured curves over the displayed operating range; this comparison concerns these implementations and recordings.

\subsubsection{Coverage, and what limits it}

No gate exceeds about \SI{20}{\percent} correct fault verdicts (the envelope rule's 373
of 1839) before false acceptance passes \SI{10}{\percent}. The comb tests, which add cepstral pre-whitening and a
record-specific null to the same kurtogram band selection, reach 323--349 below \SI{10}{\percent} false acceptance,
so low coverage persists across these implementations, which share substantial preprocessing. On CWRU, where Smith and Randall graded the diagnosability of every record \cite{smith2015}, the surrogate-null comb agrees with 8 of the 9 records they grade as textbook cases and with none of the 10 they grade as not diagnosable (Spearman 0.57), with no cross-family error and none of the 4 normal records flagged; the detector had been revised after an earlier run on these records (Supplementary~S2). Table~\ref{tab:bearings} shows where the coverage lies. At the three adaptation conditions the envelope rule certifies 4 of the
11 real-damage bearings on more than one recording: KA04 (41 of 60), KA16 (40), KI16 (32) and KI18 (45). These are the
specimens with the largest damage: extent class 2 or 3, or a pit \SI{2}{mm} long and \SI{3}{mm} wide (KA04), with the damage
spanning the full raceway width on three of them. Every bearing it does not certify has extent class 1 and damage no larger
than \SI{2}{mm} in either dimension. The two comb tests certify the same four bearings and also KI04, correctly, on 2 (alias-guarded) and
4 (surrogate-null) of its 60 recordings; their wrong verdicts fall on one and two recordings of KA30, respectively, and for
the surrogate-null comb also on 7 recordings of KI16. The raw-spectrum rule fires on every recording of those four, but also on bearings with no envelope signature and on healthy
ones. Coverage is associated with damage size in this dataset. Size, specimen, mounting and resonance are not independently varied, so this does not establish size as the sole cause or exclude improvements to the detector.

\subsubsection{Inside the adaptation loop}

Over the ten splits the envelope gate adds a median 4.5 points to bearing-disjoint
accuracy (conditional CI 0.7--22.3, positive in 9 of 10) and closes a median \SI{19}{\percent} of the
oracle gap (conditional CI 2--42). Where it acts it changes what the model says: with fold~B as source, KA04 moves from
\SI{100}{\percent} inner-race predictions to 71/29 inner/outer. The gain, however, lies almost entirely on the recordings it
certifies (Table~\ref{tab:gateacc}). Splitting each split's clean windows post hoc by gate verdict, certified windows gain a median 47 points, uncovered faulty
windows a median 0.3 (sum over splits slightly negative) and healthy windows a median 0; certified windows carry
\SI{104}{\percent} of the summed gain, so the remaining windows have a net negative contribution. This is a transductive filtering benefit, not demonstrated improvement on independent future target recordings. Because SFDA is scored on the windows it adapts on, a gain on certified windows partly reflects selective supervision on the same records used for evaluation; the gate only filters existing labels. Using the verdicts directly does more. In a post-hoc comparison, replacing the ungated model's prediction by the gate's verdict on every window of a certified recording, with no change to adaptation, adds a median 8.7 points (conditional CI 5.1--27.7, positive in 10 of 10 splits), more than the in-loop gate in every split (Table~\ref{tab:gateacc}) and, on the two folds, in each of three seeds (mean +10.3 to +10.9 against +5.7 to +8.0 points over the 12 tasks); the verdict comes from each recording alone, but the override replaces, on certified recordings, and keeps, on silent ones, the predictions of the transductively adapted model, so its measured gain belongs to the same transductive evaluation. The size of the gain therefore follows coverage closely: in a post-hoc analysis across the splits it tracks
the share of faulty target recordings the gate speaks on ($\rho = 0.95$) and the label-free share of all target recordings
it speaks on ($\rho = 0.93$), and both descriptive associations remain after partialling out headroom (oracle minus ungated clean;
partial $\rho = 0.93$ and 0.90), whereas the corresponding partial association for headroom given faulty-recording coverage is small (partial $\rho = 0.04$). The
coverage of a split is set by whether KA04, KA16, KI16 or KI18 are among its clean bearings: the in-loop gain is
$-1.5$ points with none of them, 0.7 to 4.3 with one, 3.6 to 9.3 with two, and 22.3 and 22.6 with three and four. The
ten clean targets hold 1.8 of these bearings on average, as uniform draws would, so the benefit is specimen-specific
rather than a product of the split design. On S03, where the gate speaks
on two recordings of KA30 and is right on one, gating costs 1.5 points.

On the two mechanical folds, over three seeds, the alias-guarded comb adds a mean $+4.03$ points and the envelope rule
$+7.05$, positive in every seed (Fig.~\ref{fig:gains}; per-seed summaries in Supplementary~S6); the comb was never run on the splits and no
general claim is made for it. Between seeds, the standard deviation of the per-task gain, averaged over tasks, is 1.7
points for the comb and 2.4 for the envelope rule, against 2.8 for the ungated per-task accuracy. The raw-spectrum band rule, run on the folds with one seed only, has a mean effect of $-3.9$
points that hides $+5.4$ with fold~A as source and $-13.2$ with fold~B: erroneous verdicts can retain incorrect agreeing pseudo-labels or suppress correct disagreeing ones, and the observed effect differs between target bearing sets.

\subsubsection{Does the operating point need the target rig?}

The operating point $z = 10$ was fixed before any verdict was computed, and verdicts use no target labels, but the evidence that it rarely accepts a healthy recording comes from Paderborn's own healthy recordings: its validation, though not its choice, relies on the target rig. Swept on 24 healthy recordings
from two rigs that appear nowhere in the adaptation experiments (20 from UORED and 4 CWRU baselines, truncated to
Paderborn's spectral resolution), the same rule needs $z = 36$ for zero false acceptance and accepts 4 of the 24 at $z = 10$.
On Paderborn the nearest swept point, $z = 40$, certifies 333 of 1839 fault recordings against 366 at $z = 10$
(\SI{91}{\percent} of the verdicts) with no healthy false acceptance. These measurements do not establish transfer of the operating point; it requires characterisation on healthy recordings at the deployment rig; the adaptation arms were run at $z = 10$ only.
